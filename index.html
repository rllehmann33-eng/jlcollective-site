01

In the account

We run the account ourselves, and every change we make gets written down.

The work is paid media, mostly Meta. We take the account over, plan the campaigns, and work it week to week. There's no account manager relaying messages between you and whoever's actually doing it.

We wrote the software we run accounts on. It reads the numbers, catches a Tuesday problem on Tuesday, and writes up the change it thinks should happen. Then it stops. It doesn't touch anything on its own. One of us reads what it wrote, decides, and makes the change, and that decision goes into the record with our name on it.

Your weekly report is built out of that record, so what you read is what we did.

Custom software

Sometimes the system a business needs doesn't exist. An internal dashboard that finally shows the right number, a quoting tool, a booking platform. We design it, build it, and stay on it after launch. The person who wrote it is the person you email when it breaks.

Bays, yards and docks

We stay in four trades so we're never learning your business on your money.

Four trades, and we turn down work outside them more often than we take it. Owners who know their trade cold and want the marketing run with the same seriousness. If you're outside these, write anyway and we'll tell you straight whether we're the right firm.

Automotive aftermarket

Retailers, installers, and the brands supplying them. A seasonal calendar, a long consideration window, and a buyer who's already read three forums before he calls you.

Paint protection film

A referral trade where the buyer has usually talked to two other shops before he talks to you. The job sells on finish and warranty, and the ad has to survive a comparison he runs himself.

Marine & yacht

Seasonal, regional, and slow to decide. A buyer who researches for months and then buys in a weekend.

Trucking & fleet

Owner-operators reached through national marketplaces. Large transaction sizes, an audience that's rarely at a desk, and no patience for noise.

05

The awkward ones

The questions we'd ask if we were you.

Who's actually going to be in our account?

Luke, day to day. Jack on the relationship and the creative. Write to hello@thejlcollective.com and it reaches both of us.

You're a new firm. Why hand you the account?

Because you can see exactly who'd be doing the work and hold those names to it in writing. Start with the audit. It costs nothing, it's the same document whether you hire us or not, and it'll tell you inside a couple of weeks whether we're any good at this.

Who or what is making the decisions?

The software watches and writes up what it thinks should change. Luke decides. If you want to see what it proposed and what he did with it, ask and we'll show you the log.

What does it cost?

It depends on what you spend and how much work the account takes. A monthly fee against something tied to performance, and both numbers go in front of you before you commit to anything. The audit costs you nothing.

Do you work with our competitors?

One client per market. If taking you on would be a conflict, you'll hear it on the first call.

How fast do things actually happen?

The watching and the writing-up don't stop. The only step that waits on a person is the decision, and that person is Luke or Jack. A change you ask for can go live the same day.

What happens to our data?

It stays in your advertising accounts, which you own. We work inside them with access you grant and can revoke whenever you want. We don't sell data, move it between clients, or feed it into model training. Details are in the privacy policy.

We need something custom built. Is that you?

Often, yes. Applications, marketplaces, quoting and booking systems, internal tools. Tell us what it needs to do and we'll tell you plainly whether we're the right people to build it.
